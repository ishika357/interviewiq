# HR and Behavioural Interview Knowledge

## Tell me about yourself
Structure the answer as present, past and future. Start with your current role or studies, then mention the relevant skills, projects and experience that prepared you, and finish with what you want to do next and why this role fits. Keep it to about one to two minutes.

## The STAR method
For behavioural questions describe the Situation, the Task you were responsible for, the Actions you personally took and the Result, ideally with a measurable outcome. Finish with what you learned. Use I rather than we when describing your own contribution.

## Handling disagreement and feedback
Listen to understand the other view before replying. Separate the idea from the person, support your position with data or examples, look for the shared goal and be willing to compromise. Treat feedback as information to improve, thank the person and act on it.

## Strengths and weaknesses
Choose a real strength and prove it with a specific example. For a weakness, name something genuine that is not critical for the role and explain the concrete steps you are taking to improve it.

## Communication tips for technical interviews
Think aloud, clarify the question before answering, structure the answer in a short opening, the main points and a brief summary, give a concrete example, and admit what you do not know while explaining how you would find out.

## Why this role or company
Show that you understand the role, connect your skills and projects to its requirements, and explain what you want to learn and contribute.
